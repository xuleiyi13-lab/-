# Interpretation matrix

Plan the selected cells in reading order: nine or four for portrait `3:4`, six or four for landscape `4:3`. Write each as `(anchor) + (flat role) + (one retained cue or material behavior) + (soft-edge treatment) + (density)`. Keep the plan short enough that it describes a graphic field rather than an illustrated object.

## Balanced role bank

Select one materially different role per planned cell:

1. Core reduction: keep one broad contour and one sparse signature cue.
2. Dominant curve: interpret direction, route, posture, or edge as one main curve with a small broken rhythm.
3. Negative-space channel: recognize the anchor through one cream void, gap, fork, or flow channel.
4. Cropped signature: enlarge and crop one disk, joint, profile, opening, wheel arc, or material boundary; omit the complete assembly.
5. Fragment rhythm: use unequal dots, blocks, dashes, islands, posts, windows, or waves derived from repeated parts.
6. Motion trace: show trajectory, drift, impact, or force without drawing the whole moving object.
7. Material field: reduce foam, fur, cloth, leaves, sand, smoke, cloud, or reflection to one irregular mass with controlled gaps.
8. Erosion or spill: let one field dissolve, scatter, pool, or bleed into another.
9. Shallow relation: show one overlap, contact, distance, or alignment between two reduced anchors without building a scene.
10. Scale abstraction: enlarge one signature part until it reads first as composition and second as subject.
11. Inversion: reverse field and void while retaining one grounded cue.
12. Added-subject relation: combine the requested addition with one source anchor using one reduced interaction.

## Source Transform

Choose three to five source anchors and recombine them across the selected cell count. For portrait, include four to six abstract/material-led roles and two to four semi-literal cue studies. For landscape, follow the six-cell balance below. Include one cropped signature and no more than one simplified scene fragment. Avoid a complete hardware assembly or whole photographed scene in any cell.

## Theme Compose

Derive two to five concrete theme anchors and one relation or mood. Give the major anchors at least one readable cue, then use remaining cells for curve, material, motion, erosion, negative space, inversion, or scale. Let two to four cells read immediately as stated nouns while the others broaden the theme without forming one complete scene.

## Source + Added Subject

Keep five to seven cells source-led. Use two to four cells for the added subject: one isolated reduction or cropped cue, one reduced relationship with a source anchor, and optionally one motion, material, negative-space, or scale study. Never repeat the whole added subject unchanged or render it in full detail.

## Six-cell landscape balance

For a landscape `4:3` six-grid, reuse the portrait abstraction logic at higher concentration. Choose six materially different roles from the same bank. Use four to five abstract, material, motion, erosion, scale, inversion, or negative-space studies; only one to two semi-literal cue studies; at least one cropped signature; and no more than one simplified scene fragment. Preserve only two or three signature cues across the full six-cell family.

Give every landscape cell one dominant filled mass or cream void and at most one secondary rhythm family. Aim for two to seven large countable forms, not many small descriptive marks. Convert any would-be drawing into one of these operations: merge adjacent parts into one silhouette; remove interior contours; enlarge and crop one cue; replace texture with a single broken field; replace repeated components with three to seven unequal blocks or dots. Do not use hatching, pencil strokes, engraving, fur lines, feather lines, leaf veins, tiny surface marks, or tonal modeling.

In Source + Added Subject mode, keep three to five cells source-led and use one to three cells for the addition. Include at least one reduced relationship with a source anchor when two or more addition cells are used. Never let the addition occupy more than half of the grid.

## Four-panel balance

For either four-panel layout, choose four materially different roles from the same bank. Use at least three abstract, material, motion, erosion, scale, inversion, or negative-space studies and at most one semi-literal cue study. Preserve only one or two signature cues across the complete set, with no more than one simplified scene fragment. In Source + Added Subject mode, reserve one or two panels for the addition and keep the other panels source-led.

## Balance gate

A cell is too simple when it becomes a generic curve, dot pattern, rectangle, sunburst, or logo and the full cell family no longer recovers the theme. Restore one grounded material behavior, crop, or contextual rhythm; do not automatically restore a complete object.

A cell is too literal when it renders a complete identifiable assembly, reconstructs a whole photographed scene, uses layered foreground/midground/background staging, repeats architectural openings precisely, or requires tiny linework. Replace components with a broad field, void, crop, or broken rhythm.

A landscape cell fails immediately when it resembles a sketch, colored-pencil study, engraving, or traced photograph. Reduce it until its identity is carried by silhouette, proportion, crop, contact, or one negative-space cut. Print wear may interrupt the fill but may not describe anatomy or surface detail.

## Edge gate

Every pictorial contour must specify at least one softening behavior: rounded terminal, eased corner, hand-cut wobble, absorbed ink spread, paper-fiber breakup, or luminous feathering. If the cell depends on brittle polygons, perfect repeated modules, razor-straight clipping, or CAD-clean intersections, redraw it with more organic printed edges.

## Difference gate

Two adjacent cells fail when they share the same anchor, role, crop, density, and edge behavior. Change the semantic role or retained detail first; rotation, mirroring, or color inversion alone does not create a new interpretation.

## Grounding gate

Every mark must answer which source cue, theme word, or requested added subject generated it. Avoid generic moons, stars, flowers, eyes, arrows, and sunbursts unless directly grounded. Footer icons must also be semantic: derive each one from one counted primary subject, and never add non-semantic filler marks.
